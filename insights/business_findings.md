 Business Findings

 1. Monthly Sales Performance

November 2011 generated approximately £1.51 million in qualifying sales, the highest full-month total in the observed dataset. Sales increased by 30.69% compared with October.

 2. Customer Concentration

The analysis identified 104 high-value customers, each generating at least £10,000 in qualifying sales. Together, these customers contributed approximately £3.65 million, representing 36.9% of qualifying sales linked to known customer IDs.

Two exceptional bulk transactions significantly influenced the results. Excluding these transactions reduced the number of high-value customers from 104 to 102, demonstrating the importance of outlier analysis when evaluating customer value.

 3. Exceptional Transactions

Two invoices contributed approximately £245,653 in qualifying sales. Excluding them reduced the high-value segment from 104 to 102 customers. This demonstrates the importance of validating outliers and checking whether conclusions depend on exceptional transactions.

 4. Repeat Purchasing

For complete monthly transitions, next-month retention ranged from 31.21% to 45.60%. October 2011 had the highest observed rate: 45.60% of October customers also purchased in November.

December 2011 was incomplete, so the November-to-December result should be interpreted cautiously. December-to-January retention cannot be calculated from the available data.

 Definitions and Caveats

All sales figures refer to qualifying positive sales lines in the analytical dataset, not fully reconciled net revenue. The analysis excludes certain transaction types and does not establish why sales or retention changed.


 5. Business Recommendations

1. Prepare for seasonal demand

November 2011 recorded the highest full-month sales. The retailer should investigate whether this pattern repeats across multiple years and use the findings to improve inventory planning ahead of peak demand.

2. Develop a high-value customer strategy

The 104 high-value customers contributed approximately 36.9% of qualifying sales linked to known customer IDs. The retailer should consider targeted loyalty initiatives and personalized offers to encourage repeat purchases.

3. Investigate exceptional transactions

Two unusually large invoices contributed approximately £245,653 in qualifying sales. These transactions should be reviewed to determine whether they represent genuine bulk purchases or unusual activity before using them in forecasting or customer segmentation.

4. Improve customer retention

Next-month retention ranged from 31.21% to 45.60% across complete monthly transitions. The retailer could test follow-up campaigns and repeat-purchase incentives, then measure whether they improve retention.
